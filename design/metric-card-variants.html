Which concept reads fastest at a glance

Ranked by how quickly a home screen answers "how am I doing today, and is that normal?". This is design judgement from the rendered screens, not user-test data. Run a 5-second test on the top three before committing.

#ConceptGlanceWhyTrade-off
1V1 Hero + spark tilesClosest to your current screen. The number still leads every tile, and a thin strip adds the trend without shrinking the type.Only the hero gets a D/W/M control. Tiles need the ··· menu to change range.
2V3 Wide rowsEvery metric gets your HRV card pattern: number left, chart and D/W/M right. It's one consistent reading line down the screen.The longest scroll: nine full-width cards. Nothing is prioritised.
3V6 Week strip + bulletsThe best "switch the day" model: a calendar strip anyone understands. Each bullet answers "did I hit the goal?"The bullet needs a goal. HRV and resting HR fall back to a range bar.
4V9 Focus pagerOne large chart with real axes plus a compact list. It's the fastest way to explore one metric.The overview is secondary: everything except the focused metric becomes a list row.
5V5 Synced timelineAll metrics share one time axis, so cause and effect is visible: short sleep, then low HRV, then high resting HR. Drag to scrub.Dense. Small row charts give up precision.
6V2 Chart in the glassCalm and the most on-brand. The chart becomes texture behind the number, so the number stays dominant.The chart is ambient. You feel the trend but can't read it.
7V10 Insight sentencesTells people what the number means ("9% above your baseline"). Good for new users and matches your "Wear Stēle tonight" voice.Only as good as the copy engine. Slower for people who want the numbers.

Home screen

Ship V1: it's your existing layout with a trend strip on every tile. Promote any tile to the hero slot when it's the day's story (e.g. low recovery).

Day switching

Adopt V6's week strip at the top of the home screen. It makes "switch the day" one tap and works with every concept above it.

Full view

Use V9's focus card, with axes and D/W/M plus chart-type toggles, as the screen behind every tile tap.

The chart type you pick applies to every variant, so compare concepts with the same setting. Bar suits daily totals (steps, volume, calories). Line and area suit continuous signals (HRV, resting HR, sleep score). Breakdowns in D range (score contributors, meals, exercises) always draw as bars or lollipops because they aren't time series.
