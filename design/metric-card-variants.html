Metric Card Lab

One 7-day dataset (Mon Sep 22 to Sun Sep 28, today is Sunday) drawn ten ways. Every variant plots the same nine metrics with the same numbers, so any difference you notice comes from the treatment. Cards are 350 × 120. Tap one to open its full-width detail view, or hover for daily values.

Verdict

Which variant reads fastest at a glance

Ranked by how quickly a card answers the question people open a health app with: how am I doing today compared with normal? These rankings are design judgement from the rendered cards, not user-test results. Before you commit, run a 5-second test with the top three.

#VariantGlance speedWhat the eye gets firstWhy it's fast (or slow)Where it breaks
1V4 Number + sparklineToday's value, then trend directionThe number sits where the eye lands (top-left), in the largest type. The sparkline gives one gesture of shape with no axes or grid to parse. Chart ink is about 30% of the card.No goal context. The hidden y-scale exaggerates small swings: RHR moving 51 to 59 bpm looks as dramatic as recovery moving 31% to 86%.
2V9 Bullet vs goalAm I past the goal line?Today, the week's range, the average tick and the goal marker all share one horizontal track, so "on target?" is answered by a single left-right comparison. It works best for steps, protein and sleep duration.No day-by-day trend. On metrics without a goal (HRV, RHR) it becomes a dot on a range and loses its main advantage.
3V3 Today-accent barsThe one bright bar (today)Daily metrics are discrete, so discrete bars match the data. Muting six days makes today pop through contrast, and the goal line turns each day into pass or fail. Day initials are the only axis you need.Zero-based bars flatten narrow-range metrics: RHR and HRV look nearly constant. Use it for strain, volume, steps and nutrition.
4V5 Ring gaugeHow full the ring isCompletion toward a bounded goal is read before any number (the Apple Activity effect). The accent-tinted card identifies the metric family before you read the title.Rings suit bounded targets only. For RHR the ring's range is arbitrary. The trend is pushed into a tiny strip, and nine rings in a feed look alike.
5V8 Deviation from averageUp or down vs my normalBars grow from your own 7-day average, and bright means "better", which handles lower-is-better metrics like RHR correctly. It answers "is today unusual?" quickly.People have to learn the convention once. The absolute value lives only in the header, and rest days show up as large negative volume.
6V2 Soft areaOverall trend shapeThe large stacked value plus a calm filled shape give a quick sense of direction, and the average line adds context with one hairline.The filled area on a non-zero baseline implies magnitude it doesn't have. The stacked header leaves the plot only 46px tall.
7V10 Full-bleed annotatedThe shape, then the labeled peakIt has the most visual presence. Labeled high and low points save a trip to the axis, which makes it a strong single hero card.The overlaid header competes with the data. Repeated nine times it gets loud, and in-plot labels can collide on dense weeks.
8V6 Dots in typical rangeWhich dots escape the bandThis is the right model for HRV and RHR, where "within my normal range" matters more than the raw number.The ±1 SD band is abstract for score, steps and volume. The 100% stacked sleep bars hide total duration. Two readings are needed.
9V7 Heat stripA row of numbersValues are printed in every cell, so it is precise without axes, and weekly rhythm (Wed and Sat dips) is visible.Color intensity is a weak magnitude channel, so you end up reading seven numbers. The stage heat grid needs a legend to decode.
10V1 Full-axis lineTick labels and gridlinesIt is the most exact reading, but y ticks, gridlines, day labels and a goal label consume about 40% of a 120px card before any data appears.Too much chrome for a card. It is the right treatment for the full-width detail view, where it has room.

Card default

Use the V4 frame, with the big number left and chart right, as the base card for every metric. It won because the number is read before the chart.

Swap the chart by metric type

Use V3 today-accent bars for discrete daily totals (strain, volume, steps, calories + protein). Use a V4 sparkline for continuous physiology (HRV, RHR, sleep score). Use V9 bullets where a goal is the question (sleep duration, steps).

Detail view

Use the V1 full-axis treatment, with a V6 typical-range band added for HRV and RHR. Precision belongs where there's room for it.

Sleep-stage colors were checked with a color-vision-deficiency simulator against the dark card surface. Adjacent stages stay distinguishable (worst pair ΔE 15.3), and every stacked chart also uses 2px gaps and a legend.
